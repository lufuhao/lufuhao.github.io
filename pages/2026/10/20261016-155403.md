# **New Phytologist | Henan University Reveals the Mystery of wheat BB Origin**

> 2026-10-16 15:54:03

---

> Common wheat (*Triticum aestivum* L., 2n = 6x = 42, AABBDD) possesses three subgenomes, A, B, and D, and its formation did not occur in a single step. Approximately 700,000–900,000 years ago, an A-genome ancestor (*Triticum urartu*) and a B-lineage ancestor underwent allopolyploidization to form tetraploid AABB wheat; approximately 8,500–9,000 years ago, tetraploid wheat then hybridized with *Aegilops tauschii* (DD), ultimately giving rise to today’s hexaploid common wheat. The closely related donors of the A and D subgenomes have become relatively clear, but the origin of the BB subgenome has long remained elusive. Tracing the origin of this genome has also been an important subject in wheat evolutionary research for nearly a century.

---

## **01  A Century-Old Question: Where Did the BB Genome Come From?**

> In early studies, based on chromosome pairing in hybrid progeny, researchers considered several closely related species then classified under *Agropyron* as candidates; as cytogenetic research advanced, the focus gradually shifted to the Sitopsis group of the genus *Aegilops*. Since the mid-20th century, researchers have further combined spike morphology, karyotypes, chromosome banding patterns, molecular markers, and sequence data to compare candidate species including *Aegilops speltoides*, *Ae. bicornis*, *Ae. longissima*, *Ae. sharonensis*, and *Ae. searsii*. Among these, *Aegilops speltoides* (SS) gradually became the most prominent extant candidate lineage because it showed a close relationship with BB in chromosome pairing and multiple types of molecular evidence.

> However, as research progressed, scientists found that the phylogenetic relationships revealed by different research methods and different chromosomal regions were not entirely consistent, and some genetic features of BB could not be fully explained by extant SS alone. Therefore, researchers began to consider the possibility that more than one ancestral lineage may have contributed to the evolutionary history of BB. With the advent of the whole-genome era, further research found that the ancestral BB donor lineage and the lineage of extant SS diverged approximately 4.49 million years ago, much earlier than the formation of tetraploid AABB wheat approximately 700,000–900,000 years ago. This indicates that although SS and BB are closely related, extant SS may not be the direct donor of BB; the actual diploid donor may have belonged to a closely related lineage that is now extinct or has yet to be discovered. Whether the formation of BB also involved genetic contributions from other ancestral lineages, and how these genetic components entered the ancestral BB genome, remain key questions to be answered.

## **02 Marker genes in BB genome**

> In our previous work, we sequenced, assembled and analyzed *Triticum*-*Aegilops* mitochondrial DNAs (Hu et al, 2023), Four highly variable mitochondrial genes (*atp6*, *cob*, *nad6*, and *nad9*) were stablished as marker genes for *Triticum* and *Aegilops* species identification. The diploid BB provided mtDNAs for the polyploid wheat, *Triticum turgidum* (AABB) and *Triticum aestivum* (AABBDD). And this special *atp6* contains a conserved 3'end (ATP synthase A domain) and a unique 5'end (1-377bp) (Fig 1). It has two identical copies in wheats' mtDNA, and multiple copies in BB subgenome, including 5 intact genes with full open reading frames (ORF) and 10 pseudogenes. In additation, this *atp6* was not found in either AA donor *Triticum urartu* (AA) or DD donor *Aegilops tauschii* (DD). Therefore, this *atp6* is a ideal marker gene to trace BB's origin.

## **03 New Research Progress: Henan University Finds New Evidence for St Participation in BB Formation**

> Recently, the research group led by Fu-Hao Lu at the State Key Laboratory of Crop Stress Adaptation and Improvement, Henan University, published an article entitled “A St-derived genome contributes to the formation of the wheat BB genome” online in New Phytologist. Starting from BB-specific *atp6* homologous sequences, the study combined multiple layers of evidence, including genome-wide SNPs, phylogenetic analyses, and specific genes, to identify contributions from St-related lineages to BB formation and proposed a two-step hybridization model.

## **04 From BB to St: Ancestral Genetic Clues Revealed by *atp6***

> The first clue came from the distinctive distribution of *atp6* homologous sequences among different nuclear genomes. We continued to explore BB's origin using this special *atp6* gene across species positioned along the phylogenetic tree, in reverse chronological order. More notably, among the other species compared, multiple copies of *atp6* were mainly found in polyploid species carrying the St genome (Fig. 2), for instance: *Elymus sibiricus* (StStHH), *Elymus nutans* (StStYYHH) and *Thinopyrum intermedium* (StStJrJrJvJv).

## **05 From a Single Gene to the Whole Genome: SNPs Reveal St-Derived Signals**

> A single gene provided a direction, but genome-wide evidence was needed to determine whether St-related signals were widespread. Among the 924,993 high-confidence SNPs analyzed, 813,948 (87.99%) matched the SS genome. After we filtered out these loci, we found that most of the remaining loci could be explained by St, indicating that, apart from SS, St is most closely related to BB. Further examination of 332,991 SNPs that differed between SS and St revealed that the BB genotypes at 250,169 loci matched conserved SS sites, whereas 82,822 loci matched conserved St sites, showing an approximate SS:St allele-matching ratio of 3:1 (Fig. 3). Similar signals were distributed across all BB chromosomes, suggesting that St-related genetic components are not restricted to individual chromosomal regions.

## **06  From Genetic Evidence to an Evolutionary Model: A Two-Step Hybridization Hypothesis**

> These SNP signals spanning multiple chromosomes provided a basis for tracing the ancestral composition of BB, but what evolutionary process did BB actually undergo? An ultrametric phylogenetic tree constructed from single-copy orthologous genes showed that *Aegilops speltoides* (SS) and St-related lineages diverged approximately 4 million years ago. Integrating genetic variation and lineage relationships, the study proposed a two-step hybridization model: first, an St ancestor hybridized with the common ancestor of SS and TT; subsequently, the resulting hybrid offspring backcrossed with an SS-related ancestor, ultimately giving rise to the diploid BB ancestor. Chloroplast DNA variation further supported the possibility that an SS-related ancestor provided the maternal parent in the second hybridization (Fig. 4).

## **07 Distinctive Sequence Evidence: 52 Shared Genes Retain Genetic Traces of St**

> In addition to *atp6* and SNPs, the study identified 52 BB-specific genes shared by BB and St but absent from SS. Most of these genes were lost at different stages during the evolution from the St ancestor toward the SS lineage, yet were retained in the BB genome in complete and functional forms. This distinctive gene distribution pattern is difficult to explain solely through simple linear divergence between the BB and SS lineages; instead, it further supports the possibility that the BB ancestor received genetic contributions from an St-related ancestor during its formation.

---

## **08 From a Single Donor to the Involvement of Multiple Lineages: New Insights into BB Genome Origin**

> For a long time, identifying the ancestral donor of the BB genome has been an important topic in wheat evolutionary research. Although SS-related lineages have been considered the leading candidate source of the BB genome, genetic differences between BB and extant SS indicate that its origin may have been more complex than direct inheritance from a single ancestral lineage. This study further reveals that, in addition to SS-related ancestors, St-related ancestors also made important genetic contributions to the formation of the BB genome, providing new evidence for understanding the origin of BB.

> This finding does not negate the important position of SS-related ancestors, nor does it establish St as a new sole donor of the BB genome. Rather, it incorporates St into the evolutionary framework of BB origin and proposes a two-step formation model involving ancient hybridization and subsequent backcrossing. This expands research on BB origin from the search for a single ancestral donor to exploration of the evolutionary processes through which different ancestral lineages jointly participated in genome formation through hybridization, and also provides a new perspective for reexamining the complex origin of the B subgenome of common wheat.
